## 1. Agile

Definition: Agile is a software development approach in which software is built in small parts, feedback is collected frequently, and changes are made as needed.

Example: A team develops an online shopping application by first building login, then product search, and then the shopping cart.

Use case: Projects where requirements change frequently and customer feedback is important.

## 2. Scrum

Definition: Scrum is an Agile framework that helps teams develop products through short cycles called Sprints.

Example: A team uses two-week Sprints to develop and improve an application.

Use case: Managing complex projects through regular delivery, feedback, and improvement.

## 3. Sprint

Definition: A Sprint is a fixed-length period of one month or less during which the Scrum Team works toward a specific goal and creates a usable product Increment.

Example: A team spends two weeks developing a login feature.

Use case: Breaking development into manageable periods and delivering value regularly.

## 4. Iteration

Definition: An iteration is a repeated development cycle in which a team builds, evaluates, or improves a product.

Example: The team first creates a basic search function and later improves its speed and accuracy.

Use case: Developing software gradually through feedback and refinement.

Difference: Scrum specifically calls its fixed-length cycles Sprints. Iteration is a broader term used in Agile development.

## 5. User Story

Definition: A user story describes a feature from the user's perspective, including what the user wants and why.

Format: As a [user], I want [functionality], so that [benefit].

Example: As a customer, I want to search for products so that I can find items quickly.

Use case: Understanding user needs and guiding development.

## 6. Epic

Definition: An epic is a large body of work that can be divided into smaller user stories or backlog items.

Example: A "Shopping Cart" epic can include adding products, removing products, changing quantities, and calculating totals.

Use case: Organizing large features into smaller, manageable pieces.

## 7. Story Points

Definition: Story points are a relative estimation unit used by some Agile teams to estimate the size of work, considering effort, complexity, and uncertainty.

Example: A simple validation change may be estimated at 2 points, while a complex payment feature may be estimated at 8 points.

Use case: Helping teams estimate and forecast work.

Note: Story points are not hours, and Scrum does not require teams to use them.

## 8. Acceptance Criteria

Definition: Acceptance criteria are specific conditions a user story or backlog item must satisfy to meet the agreed requirements.

Example: For a login feature:

- The user can enter a username and password.
- Valid credentials allow login.
- Invalid credentials display an error message.

Use case: Clarifying requirements and helping developers and testers verify functionality.

# Scrum Roles

## 9. Product Owner

Definition: The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing product value and managing the Product Backlog effectively.

Responsibilities:

- Defines and communicates the Product Goal.
- Creates, clarifies, and orders backlog items.
- Prioritizes work based on value and needs.
- Ensures the backlog is transparent and understood.

Example: The Product Owner prioritizes fixing a checkout problem over adding a cosmetic feature.

## 10. Scrum Master

Definition: The Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum and improving the Scrum Team's effectiveness.

Responsibilities:

- Helps the team understand Scrum.
- Facilitates effective collaboration.
- Helps address impediments.
- Coaches the team in self-management and continuous improvement.

Example: The Scrum Master helps resolve a testing delay caused by an unavailable server.

## 11. Developers

Definition: Developers are the Scrum Team members who create a usable product Increment during each Sprint.

Responsibilities:

- Plan the work needed for the Sprint Goal.
- Develop and test functionality.
- Maintain product quality.
- Adapt their plan as they learn.

Example: Developers build the login page, implement its API, and write automated tests.

## 12. Stakeholders

Definition: Stakeholders are people or groups who affect, use, fund, or have an interest in the product.

Examples: Customers, business managers, sales teams, and customer support teams.

Use case: Providing requirements, feedback, and business information to help the team improve the product.

Note: Stakeholders are not automatically members of the Scrum Team.

# Scrum Events

## 13. Sprint Planning

Definition: Sprint Planning is the event where the Scrum Team establishes why the Sprint is valuable, what can be done, and how the selected work will be accomplished.

Example: The team selects product search as its Sprint Goal and plans the work needed to deliver it.

Use case: Establishing a clear goal and an initial plan for the Sprint.

## 14. Daily Scrum

Definition: The Daily Scrum is a 15-minute event for Developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan.

Example: Developers discuss progress on the search feature and how to resolve a blocked integration test.

Use case: Coordinating daily work and identifying obstacles.

Note: It is not simply a status meeting for a manager.

## 15. Sprint Review

Definition: The Sprint Review is an event where the Scrum Team and stakeholders inspect the Sprint outcome and discuss future adaptations.

Example: The team demonstrates product search and gathers feedback about adding category filters.

Use case: Reviewing progress, collecting feedback, and adapting future work.

## 16. Sprint Retrospective

Definition: The Sprint Retrospective is an event where the Scrum Team examines how it worked and identifies improvements for future Sprints.

Example: The team discovers that code reviews are delaying delivery and agrees to review pull requests earlier.

Use case: Improving teamwork, processes, and product quality.

# Scrum Artifacts and Commitments

## 17. Sprint Backlog

Definition: The Sprint Backlog contains the Sprint Goal, the Product Backlog items selected for the Sprint, and the Developers' plan for delivering them.

Example: A Sprint Backlog contains product search, search validation, and automated tests needed to achieve the goal.

Use case: Tracking and adapting the work required to achieve the Sprint Goal.

## 18. Sprint Goal

Definition: The Sprint Goal is the single objective for the Sprint that gives the team focus.

Example: Enable customers to find products using search.

Use case: Helping the team make decisions and stay focused when plans change.

## 19. Definition of Done (DoD)

Definition: The Definition of Done is the shared description of the quality standards that a product Increment must meet to be considered complete.

Example:

- Code has been reviewed.
- Automated tests have passed.
- Required security checks have been completed.
- The feature has been integrated and works correctly.

Use case: Ensuring completed work meets consistent quality standards.

Note: If a backlog item does not meet the Definition of Done, it cannot be counted as part of the completed Increment.
